Coming Clean

Everyone has something they would rather keep hidden. Coming clean with God opens the door to forgiveness, healing, and real change.

Step Four · Coming Clean

"If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness."

1 John 1:9
Start Here

A splinter is such a small thing. You can ignore it, keep your hand in your pocket, tell everyone you're fine. But it throbs every single time you forget and actually use that hand.

And the longer it stays buried, the worse it gets, the skin closes over it, it starts to fester, and what should have been a two-second fix turns into a real infection.

Hidden sin works exactly like that. Confession is the moment you finally pull it out and name it. It stings for a second, and then, for the first time, it can actually heal.

What This Chapter Is Saying

God makes this simple on purpose

You might expect that getting right with God involves some huge, painful ritual. It doesn't. "The conditions of obtaining mercy of God are simple and just and reasonable. The Lord does not require us to do some grievous thing... but he that confesseth and forsaketh his sin shall have mercy." No pilgrimage, no self-punishment. Just honesty.

Be specific

A vague "sorry for whatever I did" isn't really confession. "True confession is always of a specific character, and acknowledges particular sins." It's actually naming the real thing, the lie, the way you treated that person, the habit you keep hiding. Specific is scary, but specific is where the freedom is.

The conditions of obtaining mercy of God are simple and just and reasonable.

Steps to Christ, chapter 4

Drop the "but"

Watch the very first confession in the Bible. Adam says, "The woman You gave me..." Eve says, "The serpent..." Both technically admit it, and both immediately shift the blame. We're experts at this, "I'm sorry, but you started it," "I know, but I was stressed." Real confession, the book says, means owning it "without deception or hypocrisy." Just you, the truth, and God, no lawyer.

As a son confesses to a loving father, so will the truly penitent bring all his sins before God.

Steps to Christ, chapter 4

Words plus a turn

Confession isn't magic words you say so you can keep doing the thing. "Confession will not be acceptable to God without sincere repentance and reformation. There must be decided changes in the life." And where you've hurt an actual person, part of confession is going to them. Apologizing, making it right where you can.

Another way to see it

Think of the relief of finally telling a friend the thing you'd been dreading to say. The words come out, the sky doesn't fall, and instantly it has half the power over you it had five minutes ago. Secrets run on darkness; the moment you say them out loud, the lights come on.

Or picture

The check-engine light. Confession isn't the mechanic yelling at you. It's just admitting the light is on so the actual repair can begin. Naming the specific problem is how it gets fixed; pretending the dashboard is blank only strands you later.

Put It Into Practice

Hiding is exhausting

"He that covereth his sins shall not prosper." There's a real, measurable weight to carrying a secret, the low-grade dread, the mental energy spent keeping the story straight, the distance it puts between you and everyone else. Hiding costs way more than we admit.

Confession is how you set it down. Telling God the specific truth, and sometimes trusting one safe person, or apologizing to someone you wronged. Breaks the secret's power over you. And you're not confessing to a judge looking for reasons to condemn you. You're talking to a Father who already knows, already loves you, and is "faithful and just to forgive." Coming clean isn't the punishment. It's the relief.

Try this today

Tell God the specific truth without excuses. If you have wronged someone, plan an honest and safe step toward making it right. If disclosure could put you in danger, speak first with a trustworthy adult or qualified professional.

Think It Through

Three questions

  1. Is there something specific you've been keeping vague with God, or with yourself?
  2. Where do you tend to add a "but" that quietly shifts the blame?
  3. Is there a person you need to actually go to and make something right with?
A Prayer

Father, I'm tired of hiding. Here's the specific truth, no excuses, no "but." Thank You that You're not waiting to condemn me but to forgive me and make me clean. Give me the courage to make things right with the people I've hurt, too.
