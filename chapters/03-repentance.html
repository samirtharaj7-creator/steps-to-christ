Step 3 of 13Repentance

A Real Change of Heart

Repentance is more than feeling bad. It is seeing sin clearly, turning from it, and letting Christ change the direction of your life.

Step Three · A Real Change of Heart

"Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out."

Acts 3:19
Start Here

You've been driving for twenty confident minutes when you glance at the map and your stomach drops, the GPS glitched, and you've been heading the exact opposite direction the whole time.

Here's the moment that actually matters. You could pull onto the shoulder and sit there berating yourself for the wasted gas and the lost time. Or you could do the one thing that helps: turn around.

That's repentance. Not parking in guilt over the wrong miles, but simply making the U-turn and driving the right way, starting from wherever you are right now.

What This Chapter Is Saying

Sorry you got caught, or sorry you did it?

The book gives a clean definition: "Repentance includes sorrow for sin and a turning away from it." Notice both halves. Feeling bad isn't enough on its own. Plenty of people in the Bible felt terrible. Esau, Pharaoh, even Judas, but they "lamented the results of sin" rather than the sin itself. They were sorry about the consequences, not the wrong.

Two kinds of "sorry"

One kind of sorry is basically fear: you're upset because you got caught, or because it's going to cost you. It fades the second the danger passes. The other kind is deeper. You actually see the thing for what it was and you want to be different. That second kind isn't something you can manufacture by trying to feel guilty enough. It's a gift.

It is the virtue that goes forth from Christ, that leads to genuine repentance.

Steps to Christ, chapter 3

You don't have to fix yourself first

A lot of people get stuck here. They think: "I have to repent hard enough before I'm allowed to come to Jesus." That's backwards. "It is the virtue that goes forth from Christ, that leads to genuine repentance." "Christ is the source of every right impulse." You don't turn in order to earn your way to Him. You come to Him, and He gives you the turning.

No earthly parent could be as patient with the faults and mistakes of his children, as is God with those He seeks to save.

Steps to Christ, chapter 3

What actually melts a heart

Guilt-tripping yourself doesn't create real change. Love does. "The goodness of God leads us to repentance." When you really see what Jesus went through, and that He did it for you, the hardness cracks. So don't wait for a better mood or a holier version of yourself. "We must come to Christ just as we are." And don't put it off: "Beware of procrastination."

Another way to see it

It helps to know the difference between regret and repentance. Regret is the sick feeling when the speed camera flashes. You're sorry you got caught, and it fades by the next exit. Repentance is deciding you don't actually want to be the kind of driver who blows through school zones, and changing how you drive.

Put It Into Practice

Guilt on a loop isn't the goal

A lot of us confuse repentance with just feeling bad. Replaying our worst moments, marinating in shame, hoping enough self-punishment will make things even. But endless guilt doesn't change anything; it just wears you down, and it can actually keep you away from God because you feel too unworthy to come back.

Real repentance is quieter and more hopeful than that. It's simply a turn. You saw where the road was going, you don't want that, so you face a new direction. And the wildest part is you don't have to arrive fixed. You turn toward a Father who is more patient with your mess than you are. Turning today beats feeling bad forever.

Try this today

Ask God to show you one sin clearly. Do not stop at feeling bad. Name the turn He is asking you to make.

Think It Through

Three questions

  1. Is there something you're "sorry" about mostly because of the consequences, not the thing itself?
  2. Have you been stuck in guilt on a loop, when what's actually needed is a turn?
  3. What would it look like to let God's kindness, not shame. Be the thing that changes you?
A Prayer

Father, I don't want to just feel bad. I want to change direction. Thank You that You give me the turning; I can't work it up on my own. Let Your kindness, not my shame, lead me home. I'm turning toward You now, right where I am.
