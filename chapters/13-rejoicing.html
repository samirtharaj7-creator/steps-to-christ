A Life of Joy

The Christian life should not advertise gloom. Remember God's goodness, speak hope, and let your life become a letter about Christ.

Step Thirteen · A Life of Joy

"These things have I spoken unto you, that my joy might remain in you, and that your joy might be full."

John 15:11
Start Here

Two friends take the exact same road trip. Same car, same route, same weather. Months later, one's camera roll is nothing but the traffic jams, the rained-out hike, and the gas station that overcharged. The other's is full of sunsets, playlists, and inside jokes.

Same trip. Same everything. The only difference is what each of them chose to point the camera at, and that choice became the memory, which became the story they tell about it.

Joy is mostly about where you aim the lens. The thorns are real; nobody's pretending otherwise. But so are the roses, and you get to decide which ones you collect.

What This Chapter Is Saying

You're a letter people are reading

Whether you realize it or not, you're representing God to everyone who knows you. "In every one of His children, Jesus sends a letter to the world." a gloomy, always-complaining faith quietly tells people a lie, that God is a hard, joyless taskmaster. But a life marked by real joy "bears false witness" against no one; it tells the truth about how good He is.

Gather roses, not thorns

The book tells a dream. A discouraged woman walks through a beautiful garden, but she's fixated on the briers snagging her, mourning that the garden is "spoiled with thorns." The guide says: "Let the thorns alone, for they will only wound you. Gather the roses, the lilies, and the pinks." Same garden, two totally different experiences, the difference is where you aim your attention. "Thank God for the bright pictures which He has presented to us."

Let the thorns alone, for they will only wound you. Gather the roses, the lilies, and the pinks.

Steps to Christ, chapter 13

Watch your words

This isn't fake positivity. It's realizing your words plant seeds in other people. "Make it a rule never to utter one word of doubt or discouragement." Every complaint you broadcast can take root in someone else's heart; every hopeful word can lift a struggling friend. So take your troubles to God in prayer, and take encouragement to people.

His heart was a wellspring of life, and wherever He went He carried rest and peace, joy and gladness.

Steps to Christ, chapter 13

Jesus was not gloomy

A lot of people secretly picture Jesus as "stern, severe, and joyless." The book pushes back hard: "His heart was a wellspring of life, and wherever He went He carried rest and peace, joy and gladness." The faith He offers "gives peace like a river... it does not cloud the sunny, smiling face." And when the weight of life presses in, you can look back and say, with the whole story behind you, "Hitherto hath the Lord helped us". He'll help you the rest of the way, too, all the way to the open gates of home.

Another way to see it

Notice that complaining and gratitude are both contagious, like a yawn in a quiet room. Spend an hour marinating in everything wrong and you can feel your own mood curdle, and everyone near you catches it. "Make it a rule never to utter one word of doubt or discouragement." Not fake cheer; just refusing to spread the gloom.

Or picture

The difference between a photo album and a scab you keep picking. Some people flip back through God's kindnesses, "the tears He has wiped away, the fears dispelled", and are strengthened for what's next. Others keep reopening old wounds until they never heal. You're always curating; the only question is what you keep on display.

Put It Into Practice

Negativity is contagious, so is joy

Complaining is basically the default setting of the internet, and it's contagious in real life too. Spend an hour marinating in outrage and cynicism and you can feel your own mood curdle. But the opposite is just as true: gratitude and hope spread, and you get to decide which one you carry into a room.

Chapter 13 makes joy a discipline, not a mood you wait around for. A few ways to practice it: keep a short list of "roses," the specific things God's actually done for you, and reread it when the thorns take over. Guard your words; before you broadcast a complaint, ask whether it plants hope or discouragement. And look backward often: remembering how God has already carried you is the surest fuel for facing what's next. You've walked all thirteen steps. Now go live them, with joy.

List five specific mercies from this week. Thank God for them, then encourage one person with the hope you have received.

Think It Through

Three questions

  1. Lately, have you been gathering roses or thorns. Where does your attention naturally go?
  2. What "bright pictures" from your own life could you list and thank God for right now?
  3. Do your words tend to plant hope or discouragement in the people around you?
A Prayer

Father, I don't want my life to tell people a lie about how good You are. Teach me to gather roses instead of thorns, to speak hope, and to carry the joy Jesus carried. Thank You for how far You've already brought me, "hitherto You have helped me," and I trust You for the rest of the way.
