Step 2 of 13The Sinner's Need of Christ

Why We Need Jesus

Self-improvement can change a few habits, but it cannot make a dead heart alive. We need more than advice. We need Christ.

Step Two · Why We Need Jesus

"I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me."

John 14:6
Start Here

Your phone hits 0% and dies mid-sentence. You can tap the screen, force-close apps, even whisper motivational speeches to it. Nothing happens. It doesn't need more effort. It needs to be plugged into a power source outside itself.

You already know this instinctively with a phone. Nobody glares at a dead battery thinking, "It just needs to believe in itself more." And yet with our own hearts we try exactly that. More discipline, more willpower, more "this time I really mean it."

Willpower is tapping a dead screen. Christ is the outlet.

What This Chapter Is Saying

Some things you can't fix yourself

We were designed for connection with God. In the beginning humans were "perfect... and in harmony with God". Pure thoughts, holy aims. Then something broke. "Selfishness took the place of love," and the wiring that connected us to God got damaged. That damage is what the Bible calls sin, and it's the reason life so often feels off even when nothing's obviously wrong.

Willpower has a ceiling

Here's the uncomfortable truth. You can be smart, disciplined, well-raised, and hard-working, and still not be able to change your own heart. "Education, culture, the exercise of the will, human effort, all have their proper sphere, but here they are powerless. They may produce an outward correctness of behavior, but they cannot change the heart." You can white-knuckle better behavior for a while. You can't self-help your way into a new person.

There must be a power working from within, a new life from above, before men can be changed from sin to holiness. That power is Christ.

Steps to Christ, chapter 2

The ladder Jacob saw

There's a scene in the book worth picturing. Jacob has just wrecked his relationships, run from home, and he's lying alone on the ground, sure his sin has cut him off from God forever. Then he dreams: a staircase joining earth to heaven, angels going up and down. That ladder, the book says, is Jesus, "the only medium of communication between God and man." When you feel most cut off, He is the bridge already reaching to you.

With His own merits, Christ has bridged the gulf which sin had made, so that the ministering angels can hold communion with man.

Steps to Christ, chapter 2

One answer for everyone

To the ache that "has gone up from burdened hearts in all lands and in all ages," there is one reply: "Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world." Only Christ can do the inside-out change, a new life "from above." And you don't have to clean yourself up before you're allowed to come. Weakness is exactly the condition He came for.

Another way to see it

It's the difference between a car that's out of gas and a car that's broken down. Out of gas, you just need a refill and you're fine. But sin isn't an empty tank. It's a cracked engine block. No grade of premium fuel fixes a problem built into the machine; you need a rebuild, and that's exactly what Christ offers.

Or picture

Trying to lift yourself off the ground by pulling up on your own shoelaces. You can strain until you're red in the face, the physics simply won't cooperate. Rescue has to come from a hand outside the hole.

Put It Into Practice

New apps won't fix a broken system

We live in the golden age of self-improvement: routines, habit trackers, productivity hacks, "become your best self." A lot of it is genuinely useful. But you've probably noticed it works for a week or two and then quietly resets. That's because most of our problems aren't at the "apps" level. They're at the operating-system level. You can't patch a heart from the outside.

Chapter 2's relief is that you were never meant to. Christ doesn't just hand you better behavior; He offers you a new heart. And the entry requirement is the opposite of what you'd expect, not "get it together first," but "come as you are, sinful, helpless, dependent." Your weakness isn't disqualifying. It's the doorway.

Try this today

Write down one part of yourself you have not been able to repair. Tell Jesus plainly that you need Him to do what willpower cannot.

Think It Through

Three questions

  1. Where have you tried to fix something in yourself by sheer willpower, and watched it snap back?
  2. When do you feel most "cut off," like Jacob? What would it mean that Jesus is already the bridge there?
  3. What are you waiting to clean up before you feel allowed to come to God? What if you came today instead?
A Prayer

Jesus, I'm done pretending I can fix myself. I need You, not just to help me behave, but to give me a new heart. Thank You for being the bridge back to the Father. I'm coming just as I am. Meet me here.
