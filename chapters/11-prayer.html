Step 11 of 13The Privilege of Prayer

Talk to God as a Friend

Prayer is not a performance or a way to inform God. It is opening your heart to Him as to a friend and learning to live in His presence.

Step Eleven · Talk to God as a Friend

"Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you."

Matthew 7:7
Start Here

You don't rehearse a formal speech before texting your best friend. You don't open with "Dear Sir or Madam" and clear your throat. You just talk. Mid-thought, half-spelled, whenever something comes up.

That easy, unguarded, say-anything closeness is the entire point. It's the difference between reading a statement at a press conference and calling someone who actually loves you.

Prayer is the call, not the press conference. It's opening your heart to a Friend who always has His read receipts on.

What This Chapter Is Saying

Prayer is just opening your heart

One line anchors the whole chapter, and it is worth writing on your wall: "Prayer is the opening of the heart to God as to a friend." That's it. Not fancy words, not a formula. And notice: "Prayer does not bring God down to us, but brings us up to Him." It changes you. Even Jesus, while He was on earth, "was often in prayer". If He needed it, so do we.

You can't bug God

Maybe you worry your problems are too small, or that you've asked too many times. Read this: "Keep your wants, your joys, your sorrows, your cares, and your fears before God. You cannot burden Him; you cannot weary Him." Nothing is too big for the One who "holds up worlds," and "nothing that in any way concerns our peace is too small for Him to notice."

Prayer is the opening of the heart to God as to a friend.

Steps to Christ, chapter 11

How to actually pray

A few things that make prayer real, straight from the book: feel your genuine need; come in faith, believing God hears; forgive others as you ask forgiveness; and keep at it, "perseverance in prayer has been made a condition of receiving." And don't skip secret prayer, just you and God: "this is the life of the soul." You can pray anywhere, "a closet of communion may be found wherever we are," even walking down a busy street.

We do not pray any too much, but we are too sparing of giving thanks.

Steps to Christ, chapter 11

Say thanks more

One gentle challenge: "We do not pray any too much, but we are too sparing of giving thanks." Prayer isn't only a wish list. Gratitude re-tunes your whole heart, "the soul may ascend nearer heaven on the wings of praise." Try ending each prayer by naming a few things you're thankful for.

Another way to see it

And you can't annoy Him with too many messages. A stranger might tire of your texts; a parent never gets tired of hearing from their kid. "You cannot burden Him; you cannot weary Him." There's no such thing as messaging God too often, or bringing Him something too small.

Or picture

Breathing. You don't schedule it, perform it, or save it for special occasions. It's the constant, quiet exchange that keeps you alive. That's what "pray without ceasing" means: not a daily event you clock, but the background rhythm of staying connected all day long.

Put It Into Practice

It doesn't have to sound holy

A lot of people freeze up at prayer because they think they need special "prayer words," or they feel silly talking to Someone they can't see. But if prayer is just opening your heart to a Friend, then the pressure's off. You can be blunt. You can be messy. You can pray in the shower, on the walk to class, in the middle of a stressful group chat.

And about unanswered prayer, because you've felt it. The book is honest: sometimes God answers by giving "that which will be for our highest good," not the exact thing we asked for, because He "is too wise to err, and too good to withhold any good thing." Trust the timing you can't see. Keep talking to Him. And add gratitude. It changes the one praying more than anything.

Try this today

Set aside five quiet minutes. Tell God what is actually happening in your life, then pause and place the day in His hands.

Think It Through

Three questions

  1. Does prayer feel like a performance to you? What would change if it were just talking to a Friend?
  2. Is there something you've decided is "too small" to bring to God?
  3. When was the last time your prayer was mostly thanks instead of mostly asks?
A Prayer

Father, thank You that I can just talk to You, no special words required. Here's my real heart: my worries, my hopes, the stuff I usually think is too small. And thank You, for more than I usually stop to notice. Teach me to keep this door open all day long.

Amen
