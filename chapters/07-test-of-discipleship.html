The Evidence of a Changed Life

A changed life does not earn salvation. It shows that Christ is at work. The clearest evidence is growing love for God and people.

Step Seven · The Evidence of a Changed Life

"If any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new."

2 Corinthians 5:17
Start Here

Your phone screen says 100%, you head out for the day feeling set, and ten minutes later it dies in your hand. The label wasn't the proof. How it actually holds up across the whole day is.

You can't judge a battery by the number it flashes at you; you judge it by whether it goes the distance. A glowing "100%" that quits at lunch is worse than an honest "60%" that lasts.

That's the test of real faith. Not the label you wear or one great spiritual high, but the pattern of your life over time. Quietly, steadily holding its charge.

What This Chapter Is Saying

You can't see the wind, but you can see what it does

Jesus compared the Spirit's work to wind. Invisible, "yet the effects of which are plainly seen and felt." You may not be able to pinpoint the exact moment you changed, and that's fine. What matters is that the change shows: "If the heart has been renewed by the Spirit of God, the life will bear witness to the fact." A real inner change leaves outer evidence.

Look at the pattern, not the moment

Here's a rule that saves a lot of anxiety: "The character is revealed, not by occasional good deeds and occasional misdeeds, but by the tendency of the habitual words and acts." One great day doesn't prove you've arrived; one bad day doesn't prove you're fake. What tells the truth is the overall direction. Where your habits are drifting over time.

Steps to Christ, chapter 7

Two ditches to avoid

The book names two opposite mistakes. One: trying to make yourself holy by sheer effort and rule-keeping, "attempting an impossibility." Two: assuming that since we're saved by grace, how you live doesn't matter at all. The truth runs between them: "Obedience, the service and allegiance of love. Is the true sign of discipleship." You don't obey to earn love; you obey because you're loved. "Obedience is the fruit of faith."

The closer you come to Jesus, the more faulty you will appear in your own eyes; for your vision will be clearer.

Steps to Christ, chapter 7

Feeling flawed can be a good sign

Counterintuitive but freeing: "The closer you come to Jesus, the more faulty you will appear in your own eyes." Not because you're getting worse, because your vision is getting clearer next to His perfection. So if you feel more aware of your flaws lately, don't spiral. That awareness can be evidence the light is on. And when you fall, "we are not cast off". Christ is your advocate.

Another way to see it

It's the difference between a costume and a character. Anyone can throw on the jersey; it takes a whole season of training and sweat to actually be an athlete. Faith you can wear as a costume, the right words, the right posts, but discipleship is what shows up when the game's on the line and nobody's clapping.

Or picture

A plastic apple in a fruit bowl. For a day it fools everyone. Same shine, same shape as the real one beside it. But give it a month: the real apple ripens, softens, drops seeds, does everything living things do, while the plastic one sits there identical to the day you bought it. Real faith is connected to a living tree.

Put It Into Practice

Highlight reel vs. real life

We're fluent in the curated version of ourselves, the post, the caption, the version we show. It's tempting to measure our faith the same way, by our best-looking spiritual moments. But real discipleship isn't about the highlight reel. It's the quiet, unposted pattern: how you talk when you're annoyed, what you do with your free time, who you are when nothing's being recorded.

So don't audit yourself by one great worship night or one embarrassing failure. Ask instead: over the last few months, which direction are my habits drifting? And if you've become more aware of your flaws, take heart, that's often what growing looks like, not proof you're fake.

Try this today

Look back over the last month. Where is Christ growing love, honesty, patience, or obedience in you? Ask Him for the next change.

Think It Through

Three questions

  1. Which way are the habits of your life drifting lately, not your best day or worst day, but the pattern?
  2. Are you closer to the "try harder" ditch or the "it doesn't matter how I live" ditch?
  3. Have you been spiraling over your flaws, when that very awareness might be a sign of growth?
A Prayer

Jesus, I don't want a highlight-reel faith. Grow real fruit in the quiet, unseen parts of my life. When I see my flaws more clearly, keep me from despair. Remind me You're my advocate, not my accuser. Let obedience grow in me as the natural fruit of being loved by You.
